## Prospective release scope

The **2026-11-04 historical scope decision** classified primary task-quality superiority over matched BF16 and at least 10% lower time-to-quality as optional research objectives. The following reaffirmation updates the product objectives while preserving that decision's historical record. Standard NF4 QLoRA may have a measured runtime cost from dequantization; slower execution alone does not establish a correctness defect. Existing experiment protocols, costs, results and original pass/fail conclusions remain unchanged; no historical result is reclassified.

On **2026-11-05**, the continuation reaffirmed improved task quality and lower training time to a fixed quality target as product objectives. The unchanged noninferiority requirements remain minimum guards; memory savings alone do not complete these objectives. The original numerical targets and historical decisions stay attached to their original studies. A new measurement must state its target, evaluation cadence, complete cost and statistical scope before outcomes; a warmup timing result or retrospective checkpoint score cannot establish an earlier observed target-crossing time. Inference acceleration remains a separate, unmeasured capability.

On **2026-11-19** the user revised two bars after the best implemented recipes were exhausted; original thresholds, results and failures stay attached to their studies:

- **Speed.** QLoRA is conventionally a memory-for-speed trade: NF4 dequantization adds per-step cost, so a time-to-quality advantage over matched BF16 LoRA is not a logical expectation when both fit. The "at least 10% lower time-to-quality" objective is withdrawn. The revised speed bar is bounded overhead: NF4 steady per-update time at most **25% above** matched BF16 LoRA, with the memory saving as the claim. Measured: Alpamayo +2-3%, Qwen3-VL +5.84%, GR00T approximately equal.
- **GR00T closed-loop success.** A practical bar of at most **10 percentage points** NF4-vs-BF16 LoRA success loss replaces the prospective 2-point guard for the release decision. The three-seed rollout (upper one-sided 95% loss 2.08 points, point loss 0.42) does not meet the original 2-point guard and meets the practical bar; no further seeds are run.

Release requires substantial memory savings (the existing matched 6 GiB device-process criterion), preserved task quality, removal of avoidable implementation overhead, and native training, checkpoint/recovery, export/reload and compatibility evidence for the advertised scope. The **2% relative NLL and one-percentage-point accuracy-loss bounds remain unchanged**, including paired seeds, recording-group resampling and both hierarchical and crossed uncertainty checks. Useful adaptation over the frozen NF4 base remains required. Applicable shared optimizations belong in both precision arms. Report residual runtime cost and complete workflow/resource measurements separately from the still-open quality-improvement and time-to-quality objectives; memory savings alone do not satisfy those objectives.

These criteria do not presently establish a release pass. The original diagnostic has an observed NLL regression; later selected point estimates improve NLL but their uncertainty bounds do not establish noninferiority. Exposed development/regression recordings cannot provide independent final confirmation. Runtime-only improvements must first pass their own numerical and recovery checks, and their measured benefit must justify their complexity and memory cost. The [unchanged quality and memory gates](#unchanged-release-gates-and-pending-measurements) and the individual result scopes below remain authoritative.
